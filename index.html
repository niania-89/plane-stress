Plane stress basics

What is stress?

Simple idea: stress is how hard the material is being pushed or pulled, per unit of area. A thin bar feels the same force as a thick bar, but the thin bar is stressed more.

stress = force / area   (σ = F / A, units N/mm² = MPa)
Direct (normal) stress σ
Acts perpendicular to a face. Tension (T) pulls away, positive. Compression (C) pushes in, negative.
Shear stress τ
Acts along a face, like scissors or sliding cards. It tries to distort the square into a diamond.

Stress elements

What is plane stress?

A thin plate loaded in its own plane has no stress through the thickness. Everything happens in the x and y directions, so three numbers describe it: σx, σy and τxy.

Stress at a joint under simple tension

A bar has σ₀ = F/A along its axis. A joint whose normal is at angle φ to the axis feels:

σ = σ₀ cos²φ    τ = σ₀ sinφ cosφ
Sign convention used on this site: tension is +, compression is −. τxy is positive when the shear on the right-hand face points up (a counterclockwise turn on the element).

Stress transformation

Simple idea: the same material state gives different σ and τ when you cut it at a different angle. θ is measured counterclockwise from the x-face.

σθ = (σx+σy)/2 + [(σx−σy)/2] cos2θ + τxy sin2θ
τθ = −[(σx−σy)/2] sin2θ + τxy cos2θ
σavg = (σx+σy)/2
σ1,2 = σavg ± √[((σx−σy)/2)² + τxy²]
τmax = √[((σx−σy)/2)² + τxy²]
tan 2θp = 2τxy / (σx−σy)
Quadrant check: do not trust a calculator's atan alone. Use atan2(2τxy, σx−σy). The angle it gives is where σ1 acts, measured counterclockwise from the x-face. The other principal plane is 90° further round.

Why 2θ? Two faces 90° apart on the element are 180° apart on the circle, so every physical angle is doubled.

How to draw Mohr's circle: step-by-step guide

Example: σx = 80 MPa (T), σy = 20 MPa (T), τxy = 30 MPa.
Sign convention: the τ axis points down, because positive τxy (counterclockwise on the vertical faces) plots down. So A = (σx, τxy) and B = (σy, −τxy) always sit on opposite sides, and a counterclockwise turn on the element is a counterclockwise turn on the circle.

Learn how to solve examination questions

Each example follows the same routine: understand, formula, substitute, solve, draw, answer, tip.

Example 1: simple direct stress at a joint

A. Understand. A bar of area 100 mm² carries 20 kN in tension. Find σ and τ on a joint whose normal is 30° from the axis.

B. Formula. Axial stress, then σ = σ₀cos²φ and τ = σ₀sinφcosφ.

C and D. Substitute and solve.
σ₀ = F/A = 20 000 N / 100 mm² = 200 N/mm²
σ = 200 × cos²30° = 200 × 0.75 = 150 N/mm²
τ = 200 × sin30° × cos30° = 200 × 0.5 × 0.866 = 86.6 N/mm²

E. Check. σ² and τ must satisfy (σ − 100)² + τ² = 100²: 2500 + 7500 = 10 000 ✓ (Mohr's circle for simple tension has centre 100 and radius 100).

F. Answer. σ = 150 N/mm² (T), τ = 86.6 N/mm².

G. Tip. Convert kN to N before dividing by mm². Say whether the angle is to the axis or to the joint plane.
Example 2: combined stress, principal and maximum shear, Mohr's circle

A. Understand. σx = 80, σy = 20, τxy = 30 MPa. Find σ1, σ2, τmax and the stresses on a plane at θ = 30° counterclockwise from the x-face.

B and C. Formulas with substitution.
C = (80 + 20)/2 = 50 MPa
R = √(30² + 30²) = 42.43 MPa

D. Solve.
σ1 = 50 + 42.43 = 92.43 MPa, σ2 = 50 − 42.43 = 7.57 MPa, τmax = 42.43 MPa
2θp = atan2(60, 60) = 45°, so θp = 22.5° counterclockwise from the x-face to σ1
σθ = 50 + 30cos60° + 30sin60° = 50 + 15 + 25.98 = 90.98 MPa
τθ = −30sin60° + 30cos60° = −25.98 + 15 = −10.98 MPa

F. Answer. σ1 = 92.4 MPa, σ2 = 7.6 MPa, τmax = 42.4 MPa, σθ = 91.0 MPa, τθ = −11.0 MPa (clockwise).

G. Tip. Check σ1 + σ2 = σx + σy (100 = 100 ✓). Marks are lost for a wrong rotation direction and for forgetting to halve 2θ.
